# Transcript store

The single durable component of the network. A **transcript** is an
append-only log of positioned entries under one scope; the store
holds every transcript in the system. There is no separate
sequencer, record store, or mailbox component — sequencing is the
append side of this contract, records are its read side, and an
agent's inbox is just one more transcript.

## Operations

```
interface TranscriptStore
  append(scope: ScopeId, entry: Entry) -> Commit
  read(scope: ScopeId, after: Seq, limit) -> Page
  head(scope: ScopeId) -> Seq
  release(scope: ScopeId, upTo: Seq) -> ()

Commit = SeqCommit                      -- { scope, seq, entryHash, seqSig }
Page   = { items: Committed[], head: Seq }
Committed = { entry: Entry, commit: Commit }
```

`scope` is the generic form of `conv`: a conversation transcript's
`ScopeId` is its `ConversationId`, and the `SeqCommit` shape in the
[packet appendix](../packets) is this `Commit` with the scope
specialized.

### `append`

- **Pre.** Caller is authorized to append to `scope` (a deployment
  rule, below).
- **Post.** The entry is durable (P-LOG.1); the returned `Commit`
  binds it to position `head+1`, maintaining consecutive unique
  binding (P-LOG.2) and non-repudiable assignment (P-LOG.3). The
  party issuing commits for a scope is that scope's **sequencer** —
  a role within this contract, not a component; v0: the store
  itself.
- **Refusal.** Authority, quota, or PCC once #765 binds it; failure
  taxonomy is open question #9.

### `read`

- **Pre.** Caller is authorized to read `scope` (deployment rule;
  history-read scope is open question #7).
- **Post.** A prefix-complete continuation from `after` (P-LOG.4) of
  immutable committed pairs (P-LOG.5), verifiable by the caller
  regardless of who served it (P-LOG.3).

### `head`

- **Post.** The scope's highest committed position. Comparing it to
  a held high-water mark makes withheld entries detectable as gaps
  (P-LOG.2).

### `release`

- **Post.** Retention for positions `≤ upTo` of `scope` is
  released, where the deployment permits it — the reader-side bound
  on the retention window (P-LOG.5). Post-release retention: open
  question #7.

## Deployments

| Deployment | Scope | Entry | Appends | Reads | Releases |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Conversation transcript | `ConversationId` | `LogEntry` | members, under authority rules chartered to #765 | members (history scope: #7) | governed by #7, not by readers |
| Inbox transcript | recipient `AgentId` | `Datagram` | the store, on accepting a datagram for that recipient | the recipient | the recipient, after processing |

Authorization is a property of the deployment, not of the
interface. The inbox transcript is the **discovery root**: the one
scope an agent knows from registration, through which it learns of
conversation scopes it does not yet know (bootstrap invites).

## Delivery

Push is unpromised; reads are authoritative. On commit, the store
MAY push `Committed` values to the scope's recipient set (P-MEM.3)
over the transport (P-DELIV.3: no promise); a recipient recovers
completely by `read` from its held position. This division is what
derives the delivery properties instead of owning them:

| Property | Derivation |
|---|---|
| P-DELIV.1 durable before delivery | `append`/accept is durable first (P-LOG.1) |
| P-DELIV.2 at-least-once | push (lossy) ∪ read (prefix-complete, P-LOG.4) covers every accepted item; overlap makes duplicates normal (R-EP.5) |

## Decision needed: what inbox transcripts durably hold

Two consistent designs; this page recommends (b), and the choice
needs a recorded maintainer decision:

- **(a) Inbox-primary.** Every conversation entry is durably
  appended to every member's inbox transcript. One recovery path —
  but an N-member conversation stores N+1 durable copies of every
  entry, and inbox retention grows with conversation history.
- **(b) Stream-primary (recommended).** Sequenced conversation
  traffic recovers via `read` on the *conversation* scope:
  reattach-resume plus gap detection (P-LOG.2) makes silent push
  loss discoverable without a durable inbox copy. Inbox transcripts
  durably hold only **unsequenced** datagrams — at v0, essentially
  the conversation-bootstrap invite to an agent not yet in the
  stream. Inbox retention is short (until `release`), storage is
  written once per entry, and the record substrate stays exactly the
  conversation transcripts (clause 13: storage is
  durable-then-deliver and the store is the record substrate).

Under (b), the endpoint's delivery state reduces to a cursor map —
one high-water mark per scope it belongs to, rooted at the inbox
transcript as the discovery channel.

## Implementation note (non-normative)

v0: one router-held store; `append` assigns a dense per-scope
counter at commit time, and commits carry the sequencer's signature
(`seqSig`) as the P-LOG.3 mechanism; a pump pushes new commits and a
per-reader cursor drives re-push on reattach. v1 assigned sparse
time-based ids ("snowflakes") *before* commit, producing a
gap-on-resume defect measured in the v1 audit (`v2/inputs/`), and
had no durable inbox at all — push was fire-and-forget and recovery
was a client-paged cursor. Decentralized later: a scope's sequencer
role moves to a member leader or quorum while storage stays wherever
it is — `read` can be served by any holder of the committed pairs,
since P-LOG.3/4 make responses verifiable regardless of who serves
them.
