# Ledger

The sequencer and record-store roles exposed as one interface:
`append` is the sequencing act, `read` is the record act. Every
durable ordered thing in the system is a deployment of this one
interface — the conversation stream is a ledger of `LogEntry`, and
the [mailbox](./mailbox)'s inbox is a small ledger of datagrams.

## Operations

```
interface Ledger
  append(scope: LedgerId, entry: Entry) -> Commit
  read(scope: LedgerId, after: Seq, limit) -> Page
  head(scope: LedgerId) -> Seq

Commit = SeqCommit                      -- { scope, seq, entryHash, seqSig }
Page   = { items: Committed[], head: Seq }
Committed = { entry: Entry, commit: Commit }
```

`scope` is the generic form of `conv`: a conversation deployment's
`LedgerId` is its `ConversationId`, and the `SeqCommit` shape in the
[packet appendix](../packets) is this `Commit` with the scope
specialized.

### `append`

- **Pre.** Caller is authorized to append to `scope` (a deployment
  rule, below).
- **Post.** The entry is durable (P-LOG.1); the returned `Commit`
  binds it to position `head+1`, maintaining consecutive unique
  binding (P-LOG.2) and non-repudiable assignment (P-LOG.3).
- **Refusal.** Authority, quota, or PCC once #765 binds it; failure
  taxonomy is open question #9.

### `read`

- **Pre.** Caller is authorized to read `scope` (deployment rule;
  history-read scope is open question #7).
- **Post.** A prefix-complete continuation from `after` (P-LOG.4) of
  immutable committed pairs (P-LOG.5), verifiable by the caller
  regardless of who served it (P-LOG.3).

### `head`

- **Post.** The scope's highest committed position. Comparing it to
  a held high-water mark makes withheld entries detectable as gaps
  (P-LOG.2).

## Deployments

| Deployment | Scope | Entry | Who appends |
|---|---|---|---|
| Conversation stream | `ConversationId` | `LogEntry` | members, under authority rules chartered to #765 |
| Mailbox inbox | recipient `AgentId` | `Datagram` | the mailbox, on accept |

Authorization for `append` and `read` is a property of the
deployment, not of the interface. The mailbox's `accept` and `drain`
are the inbox deployment's `append` and `read`; its `ack` bounds the
inbox's retention window (P-LOG.5).

## Implementation note (non-normative)

v0: one router-held store; `append` assigns a dense per-scope
counter at commit time, and commits carry the sequencer's signature
(`seqSig`) as the P-LOG.3 mechanism. v1 assigned sparse time-based
ids ("snowflakes") *before* commit, which produced a gap-on-resume
defect measured in the v1 audit (`v2/inputs/`); commit-time dense
positions are the corrective. Decentralized later: a scope's
sequencer moves to a member leader or quorum; `read` can be served
by any holder of the committed pairs, since P-LOG.3/4 make the
response verifiable regardless of who serves it.
