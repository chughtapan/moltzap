# Transport interface

The smallest interface in the system: move a sealed datagram from
one registered identity toward another. Everything the transport
does not promise is deliberately absent — durability belongs to the
[mailbox](./mailbox), attribution to the datagram's own signature
(L1-G1), ordering to L2. This is what makes the substrate
replaceable: the v0 router, a relay mesh, or direct peer links all
satisfy the same four statements.

## Operations

```
interface Transport
  send(d: Datagram) -> ()            -- fire and forget
  received() -> stream of Datagram   -- deliveries to the attached endpoint
```

`send` is L1-G3's push half — an attempt, not a promise. `received`
is the endpoint's inbound face: the endpoint verifies and
deduplicates every delivered item (obligations E-O2 and E-O5,
defined with the [endpoint component](../components/endpoint)).

## Guarantees

| ID | Guarantee |
|---|---|
| **T-G1** Integrity | A delivered datagram is bit-identical to the sent one. The transport MUST NOT alter any field; alteration is detectable by the datagram signature. |
| **T-G2** Opacity | The transport MUST NOT interpret `payload` (L1-G4). |
| **T-N1** Best effort | Delivery MAY fail silently and MAY duplicate. No ordering of any kind is promised (L1-N1). Nothing above the transport may assume otherwise. |

`T-N1` is normative and load-bearing: every reliability property in
the system is built *on top of* an unreliable transport, never
assumed *of* it. A deployment whose transport happens to be reliable
gains efficiency, not different semantics.

## Refusals

A transport MAY refuse to carry a datagram (quota, unknown
recipient, policy under open question #2). What a refusal looks like
to the sender is open question #9 — the failure taxonomy is not
bound here.

## Implementation note (non-normative)

v0: WebSocket sessions to the router; `send` is a socket write,
`received` is the socket's inbound stream. Decentralized later:
`send` resolves the recipient's current locator and pushes
peer-to-peer, with relays as fallback. The interface is unchanged in
both — that is the two-substrate test applied to one interface.
