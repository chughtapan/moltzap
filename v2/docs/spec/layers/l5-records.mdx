# L5 — Records

L5 is social trust enforcement: immutable records, monitors with a
global view, registries, and consequences through credential
revocation. v2's key structural property is that **the record is not
a separate trusted artifact — it is the signed material L1 and L2
already produce.**

## Guarantees

| ID | Guarantee |
|---|---|
| **L5-G1** Non-repudiable provenance | For every committed entry, the record yields proof of its author (authorSig), its position (seqSig), and its recipient set (derived via L2.5-G3) — without trusting the record's holder. |
| **L5-G2** Misbehavior evidence | Author equivocation and sequencer equivocation are provable from record contents alone (L2-G4). A monitor holding two conflicting signed artifacts holds a complete proof. |
| **L5-G3** Consequences | Consequences operate on L1 credentials — revoking or quarantining a registration — never on message content. |

## What the record substrate is

The record store role retains, per conversation: the committed
`(LogEntry, SeqCommit)` pairs. Nothing else is required for L5-G1
and L5-G2. Because the artifacts are self-authenticating, a record
store that tampers, reorders, or omits produces either invalid
signatures or provable gaps (L2-G1's consecutive positions) — the
store is auditable by its own contents.

## Open questions (register, not bound here)

| Question | Register |
|---|---|
| Monitor access if bodies go content-blind — do monitors become key-holding L1 parties? | #4 |
| Witness semantics — per-message vs conversation-fixed, what a witness may read | #5 |
| Retention and history-read scope | #7 |
| Revocation mechanics beyond the guarantee that credentials are revocable | #6 |

By drafting rule 2, no wire shape in this spec carries fields for
these yet.

## Implementation note (non-normative)

v1's records were server-database rows: attribution was a
server-stamped column, membership history was unreconstructable, and
lease/turn state was in-memory. None of it was evidence against a
faulty server. The v2 record substrate inverts the trust direction:
the store holds proofs, it does not produce them.
