# L3 — Guardrails

L3 is each agent's own protection: structural and semantic screening
of inbound traffic, and norm-adherent shaping of outbound traffic,
derived from that agent's personal trust — its own experience and
deployment context. **L3 has no wire footprint and no network-side
enforcement.** The router enforces none of it.

## Position in the stack

- **Inbound**: after L2 verification, before the harness's model sees
  content. Structural screening (schemas, task-specific formats,
  access rules from personal trust) and semantic screening.
- **Outbound**: send-when-expected, norm-adherent responses, as
  configured by the pinned L4 skill.

## What L3 MAY rely on from below

Screening logic may assume, without re-deriving:

| From | Fact |
|---|---|
| L1-G1 | the datagram sender is who it claims to be |
| L2-G5 | the entry author is who it claims to be, regardless of relay |
| L2-G1 | every member saw the same entries in the same order |
| L2.5-G2 | the membership at the entry's position, derived from the stream |
| L2-G4 | equivocation, if present, is provable — screening can demand proofs |

Everything else — whether the body is well-formed for the task,
whether this author should be speaking now, whether the content is
trustworthy — is L3's own judgment against the pinned L4 skill and
local trust data.

## Contacts are local data

An agent's contacts are its own trust records, consulted during
screening. They are not network objects: no contact RPCs, no
network-side contact graph, no router reachability gate derived from
them. (Whether the router retains any *spam-control* role between
strangers is open question #2 — that question is about the router,
not about contacts.)

## Violation responses are agent-local

Disregard, withdraw, pursue the goal otherwise, report evidence to
L5, seek reparations. None of these require network cooperation,
which is what makes them credible against a faulty network.

## Implementation note (non-normative)

v1 kept a network-side contacts table that gated task invitations
and derived agent visibility — trust decisions made by the router on
the agent's behalf. v2 moves the trust data to its owner. The arena
case study's regex-based message classification is the anti-pattern
L3 structural screening replaces: screening keys on skill-defined
schemas in the body, never on prose.
